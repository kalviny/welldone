## Direct comparison with round 2

- **Improved:** the entrance ceiling has fewer busy high-contrast clouds; conspicuous diagonal timber scratches are reduced; floor joints no longer disappear into cool gray; rock faces are less sharply divided into light and dark triangles. The rocks are the clearest small material improvement.
- **Regressed:** broad entrance posts and the front beam have acquired repeated transverse dark marks and boxy grain intervals. This is especially obvious along the upper beam and both broad posts. The same treatment becomes much more distracting on the staircase and study table. It trades one artificial texture direction for another visible repetitive pattern.
- **Regressed:** green crowns are smoother and more uniformly filled than round 2. The left crown and low central crown now expose large stretched scallops with very little small-scale leafy breakup. The target has a much richer hierarchy of uneven leaf clusters, overlaps, and pale interruptions. Round 2 was still too puffy, but it retained more internal pigment variation.
- **Mixed:** blossom texture is somewhat less deeply crumpled, which helps, but the current large peach-pink sheets remain broad and opaque. They lack both the target's fine coral overlap marks and its near-paper highlights. This is not a substantial foliage breakthrough.
- **Mixed:** the ceiling and walls are quieter, but the quieter material is also softer and blurrier. Reduced noise is useful; it has not produced the target's fine dry paper surface or clear separation of pale paper from selected wash pools.

The entrance difference is small enough that I would not present 6.9 versus 7.0 as a precise measurement. The recommendation to select round 2 is firmer because the full-size stairs and study images reveal substantial shared-texture problems in round 3.

## Remaining target gaps by category

### Composition — preserve the established scene

- Keep the current camera, FOV, architecture, doors, tree positions, branch structure, pond outline, and rock placements. Large component alignment is already the strongest category.
- Within those fixed crown envelopes, the target has many unequal small scallops and interrupted overlaps. Current long smooth lobes make the foliage read as fewer larger layers. Any future foliage work should change this internal and edge rhythm without moving the trees or expanding their overall silhouette.
- Preserve genuine navigable 3D volume. A reference overlay or camera-facing replacement would not resolve the appearance from the other saved views and would not qualify as target reconstruction.

### Lighting / color — local separation remains weak

- The target foreground floor has generous light peach/cream reserves interrupted by warm ochre pools and soft shadows. Round 3 is a more continuous beige-brown field with large hazy streaks. Recover pale warm areas and varied pigment density locally, without globally whitening the walls or water.
- The target front beam has bright peach/orange areas, quieter reserves, and selected richer edges. Current beam and posts remain an almost continuous medium warm stain. Stronger uniform grain contrast is not a substitute for that color structure.
- Current green foliage has a shallow yellow-green top-to-bottom transition. The target combines distinctly pale yellow-green upper passages with muted gray-green lower pockets and selected darker overlaps. Keep these regions spatially irregular rather than applying the same vertical gradient to every pad.
- The lawn still reads as an olive rectangular bed with diffuse strips. The target has broad warm dry areas and specific soft gray-green shade spreading beneath and left of the near trunk, plus contact grounding around trunks and rocks. Make shade follow those locations rather than increasing whole-ground contrast.
- The overhead blue-gray is closer in quietness, but its pools remain broad soft clouds. The target leaves clearer near-white paper interruptions and locally sharper pigment boundaries while keeping the overall light exposure.
- Spring blossom needs pale peach paper breaks and concentrated salmon/coral overlaps. The current dusty pink sheet is too even and lacks that luminous range.

### Materials — principal blocker

- **Timber:** remove the regularly spaced transverse bars, rectangular dark islands, and repeating loops visible on the front beam and posts. Use unequal, interrupted fibers along the member axis, broad diffuse wash, and selective pooled edges. The target's wood does not resemble a regular grain decal tiled over each member. The secondary staircase and tabletop show why simply rotating or sharpening this pattern is insufficient.
- **Foliage:** current green clumps are smooth solid cloud cushions with coarse soft mottling. Retain irregular macro placement, but introduce several unequal scales of broken leafy marks, selective dark overlap edges, small pale gaps, and less uniformly rounded contours. Tiny uniform speckle added on top would leave the basic pillow appearance unchanged.
- **Blossom:** break the broad opaque pink slabs with uneven small petal-like overlaps and pale dry areas. Avoid returning to the baseline's deep folded-fabric creases or round 1's regular padded rows.
- **Floor:** current wide blurry washes and repeated dark lanes do not match the target's warm, irregular pigment pools with fine paper tooth. Keep the useful warm seam color while decoupling seams from the painted wash structure.
- **Ceiling and walls:** maintain the reduction in visual busyness, but restore a distinction between broad pigment fields and quiet fine paper texture. The actual image remains visibly soft compared with the target; adding another full-surface noisy coating would repeat the earlier problem.
- **Bark:** additional mottling gives the trunk more variation, but it still lacks the target's broken vertical warm marks and pale irregular edges. Avoid repeated loop/crossbar marks shared with structural wood.
- **Rocks:** keep the softer face transitions. Their outer polygon contours and fairly uniform white mass remain evident; the target uses chalky pale gray washes that cross faces and define selected irregular edges.

### Details — secondary to the broad material gaps

- The target has finer, ragged canopy and blossom edges, restrained internal leaf/petal strokes, and small unpainted interruptions. These are still sparse or absent.
- Floor seams are now more useful, but appear quite mechanically continuous and uniform in color. The target uses restrained warm lines that vary slightly with the surrounding pigment.
- Pond reflections remain cleaner and more mechanical than the target's soft irregular blue-green paint boundaries. Rock/trunk contacts need more natural grounded marks.
- Small architectural features are already well covered. Extra props or geometry would not address the dominant mismatch.

- **Tea:** largely stable in the contact sheets. The existing saturated orange platform fascia and strongly dark left timber remain visually apart from the quieter peach building surfaces. I do not see a large new tea-view failure.
- **Lookout:** major roof, mountain, and balcony framing remain stable. The grazing foreground still has elongated soft surface structure. There is no comparable large new failure here to the study/stairs.

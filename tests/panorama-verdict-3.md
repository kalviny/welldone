## Changes that helped

- **The reduced hard shadows are the clearest lighting improvement.** Right-side trees and the foreground pine no longer stamp conspicuous crisp silhouettes onto the ground. The result is closer to diffuse watercolor lighting.
- **Pigment variation now belongs visibly to object surfaces.** The near pine and central yellow/orange tree show more internal value variation; retaining-wall stones no longer have almost uniformly flat centers. This addresses part of the prior round's central criticism.
- **Roof color and line contrast are better controlled.** The lighter, grayer blue is closer to the target, particularly across the broad lower roofs. Lines no longer dominate quite as strongly.
- **Framing is modestly more accurate.** The bridge no longer projects quite as far down and left, and the tower's top aligns more closely with the target.
- **The entrance is quieter.** Reducing the bright interior objects removes a visible distraction without harming the main structure's readability.

## What regressed or did not deliver the expected gain

- **Some foliage lost local clarity while gaining texture.** The central orange/yellow crown and near pine have more mottling, but still form long connected cushions. The new texture does not create the target's separate overlapping branch-supported clusters or its pale translucent pockets. The effect can look like mottled clay rather than paint.
- **Water is more textured but not more convincingly reflective.** Broad cloudy cyan variation dominates. The reference contains broken light/dark reflections associated with the wall and bridge, interspersed with cream paper and short irregular ripples. Unanchored mottling cannot substitute for those spatial relationships.
- **The overall image is slightly grayer and more uniformly hazy.** The target is soft, but it also has richer ochre/rust accents and more selective dark teal/gray punctuation. Softening alone reduces some of that separation.
- **Paving still reads as a regular field of small units.** The size adjustment does not yet establish the target's large irregular cream-gray slabs, interrupted joints, and strong local light variation near the central tree and gate.

## Concrete remaining gaps, in order of visual impact

1. **Tree structure and pigment hierarchy.** The target's near pine is divided into distinct overlapping clouds with visible gaps, branch forks, and deep cool pockets. The current pine is a thick continuous shelf. The central autumn tree also needs several distinct crowns with cream-yellow highlights and localized rust shadows; changing texture amplitude across the existing connected mass would leave the silhouette mismatch intact.
2. **Roof mark-making.** Broad roof faces still consist of a uniform blue-gray field and regularly spaced linear seams. The target has broken, sometimes barely visible tile boundaries, unequal strokes, pale irregular patches, and selective dark eaves. The gate roof is an especially conspicuous comparison point because of its size and front-facing placement.
3. **Stone and courtyard specificity.** Retaining-wall blocks remain repeated rounded shapes with similarly complete joints. The reference has chipped angular facets, partial joints, uneven large stones, and warm/cool patches within each stone. Courtyard slabs need irregular boundaries and a less grid-like rhythm. More global roughness would not resolve either mismatch.
4. **Reflections with recognizable placement.** The moat should show broken darker wall/bridge echoes and lighter openings, localized below the corresponding structures. The current cloud pattern is broadly even across the water and does not convey the target's reflected architecture.
5. **Background wash and contour.** The left mountain needs more definition; several distant ridges should dissolve selectively rather than all having smooth, evenly filled outlines. The target has appreciably more blue-gray cloud wash across the upper sky. Paper texture alone leaves that area too blank.
6. **Timber and bridge detail.** Bridge decking needs independently visible plank divisions and uneven warm marks. Main timber can use selective variation in darkness and thickness; the current repeated clean posts and rails remain conspicuously regular.

The strongest achievement is the close overall spatial reconstruction of the building, gate, bridge, and autumn planting in a live 3D scene. The remaining quality gap is principally the organization of painted shapes and marks, with tree geometry the largest structural exception. Third-round material changes move in the right direction, but the dominant reading remains a textured 3D diorama rather than the target's watercolor illustration.

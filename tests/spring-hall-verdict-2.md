## Remaining gaps by visual importance

**1. Shared surface treatment still obscures the target's hierarchy of marks.** The target uses broad quiet washes, irregular pale paper reserves, selective dark pigment pools, and subordinate fine paper tooth. Round 2 often gives all of these surfaces a continuous mottled coating at similar visual density. The foreground floor has long cloudy streaks; the front beam and near posts have conspicuous slanted crossing scratches; the ceiling is covered with relatively dense cyan-gray clouds. Changing the amount of this coating will not create the missing distinction between unpainted paper, pigment edges, and directional fibers.

The source supports an architectural diagnosis: the shader samples the same RGB swatch at three scales, and those swatches already contain paper texture and directional marks. Their fine detail therefore travels through world/material coordinates as well as through the final screen-space paper term. The broad timber field uses an oblique world projection, so source marks in that field can cross the structural axis. The target's near-white reserves are also difficult to obtain from the existing reserve mixes: wood is mixed toward ochre, and foliage is mixed toward a slightly brighter version of its own albedo. This is more than a missing noise-strength adjustment.

**2. Foliage still reads as large opaque pillows.** Round 2 breaks the rows, but many individual units remain broad smooth lobes with a fairly uniform cloudy fill. The target's crowns combine several sizes of irregular scallops, much finer broken leafy/petal marks, selective overlap edges, small light gaps, and a stronger top-to-bottom pigment hierarchy. The current left crown is mostly olive midtone; the target has pale yellow-green upper passages and gray-green lower pockets. Current pink is warmer, but its masses lack the target's pale peach paper breaks and more concentrated salmon pools. Keep the existing tree placements and overall silhouettes; work inside those envelopes.

**3. Local light/color structure is still flattened.** Target grass has broad warm dry areas and irregular gray-green shade extending beneath and left of the front trunk. Round 2 removes the bad stripes but leaves a largely level olive rectangle with blurred variation. The target front beam has richer orange/ochre pigment boundaries, posts have pale warm vertical breaks, and the floor has generous light peach reserves. The current wood is lighter than the baseline but stays much more evenly colored. Stronger global exposure or contrast would also change already-correct cream walls and blue roof; this needs material- and location-specific structure.

**4. Secondary marks still expose simplified 3D surfaces.** Trunks have a broad brown fill instead of broken vertical bark pigment and pale edges. Rocks retain obvious triangular light/dark patches rather than a chalky wash bridging the faces. The pond remains cleaner and more uniformly turquoise than the target, with relatively mechanical reflection streaks and few irregular blue-green paint boundaries. Foreground slab seams have shifted from overly dark to faint gray; the target uses restrained but readable warm ochre lines. These are worthwhile only after the large surfaces and foliage improve.

## Focused plan for the last, third round

This is the **stall-approaching** point under the Pro criteria: two rounds have gained only 0.5 points over the documented baseline, and foliage/paint hierarchy have been named repeatedly. Use the final round for a coherent shared-material change rather than another increase in noise, another small palette multiplier, or more similarly sized clumps.

1. **Separate pigment structure from paper texture in the shared material system.** Derive or author a low-frequency pigment field without its embedded fine paper/scratch detail. Give each material a broad wash/reserve mask and selective pigment-edge mask; use one quiet fine paper layer at a controlled screen scale. Preserve the corrected roof coordinates. For timber, keep narrow fibers along the actual member axis and ensure the broad field contains diffuse wash only, not oblique directional scratches. For ceiling, create a few broad blue-gray pools separated by much quieter pale paper areas; avoid filling every region with equally busy clouds. For floor, use slow warm peach/cream pools and restrained warm seams. Check entrance, forecourt, stairs, study, and lookout immediately after this shared change.

2. **Add a smaller-scale foliage layer and stronger painted hierarchy inside the current envelopes.** Preserve the new irregular macro-clump placement, but break their smooth edges with unequal smaller scallops and irregular leafy/petal marks. Use several mark sizes, interrupted overlap contours, and occasional light gaps; avoid uniform rims, center-darkened grapes, or another regular stack of discs. Make upper spring leaves visibly pale yellow-green and lower pockets muted gray-green. Keep blossom highlights pale peach/coral and concentrate warmer salmon pigment in selected overlaps. Retain actual 3D volume and convincing edges from the garden/approach views.

3. **Art-direct broad local light and reserve fields.** Add soft irregular tree/contact shade that follows the trunks and rocks, with warm dry lawn areas between it. Bring back pale peach reserves on floor and posts and orange/ochre pooling on selected beam edges. Use spatial masks or object/vertex pigment regions so these fields remain anchored while navigating. Do not obtain this by globally darkening the scene or reviving the autumn blue-gray bands.

4. **Spend remaining detail time on bark, rocks, and seam readability.** Broken vertical trunk marks and chalky washes bridging rock facets will help more than generic extra grain. Restore a little warm definition to the foreground floor seams and stair tread edges without returning to the dark grid of round 1. Pond refinements are lower priority than the first three items.

## Shared-style and autumn regression check

- **Ceiling/roof:** entrance and forecourt improve compared with round 1's pronounced directional streaks. The material is still too continuously busy. The grazing foreground surface in the lookout also retains elongated paint structure; the coordinate fix has not made embedded source detail behave like the target's fine paper tooth.
- **Wood/floor:** near timber now displays diagonal crosshatching more clearly, including the broad entrance posts and beam. Study-table grain is less severely stretched than round 1 but still has a soft crosshatched coating. Stair treads and floor joints have become less distinct as the floor treatment was softened. Preserve the lighter palette while correcting these mark and seam issues.
- **Ground:** the approach and garden confirm that the conspicuous parallel grass bands are much reduced. This is a clear improvement. The remaining issue is flatness and lack of spatially grounded shade, not a need to restore those bands.
- **Tea view:** it remains visually consistent between rounds; no major new regression is evident in the saved contact sheet. The left platform's saturated orange fascia still sits apart from the quieter peach painted architecture, an existing secondary mismatch.
- **Autumn ground:** round 2 substantially improves on round 1's strong blue-gray/cream streaks beside the pond. It is calmer and closer to the baseline's subdued ground. Avoid reintroducing contrast to the whole grass material when adding localized tree shade.
- **Autumn foliage:** the regular padded rows are reduced, but the new units look larger and puffier at close range. The canopy remains predominantly lemon yellow with weak ochre/pigment separation, rather than recovering the baseline's warmer golden character. This is a mixed shape change and a persistent color/material deficit, not evidence of a new large seasonal hue failure. Keep seasonal palette controls separate when refining spring coral and leaf highlights.
- Pond/rock layouts and secondary-view framing remain visually stable. I inspected saved images only; the parent's reported eight-view/runtime pass is outside this independent visual verification.

The last round should visibly change the **organization of paint**: quiet paper versus selected pigment, directional timber versus diffuse wash, and fine leafy edges versus large clumps. That offers substantially more room for improvement than further tuning the same continuous texture coating.

## Round 2 priorities

1. **Rework the foliage marks and spring palette together.** This is the most conspicuous remaining subject mismatch. The round-1 green crowns have similar-size oval units in tidy rows; the blossom has similarly regular padded discs. The target uses overlapping, irregularly scalloped leafy/petal masses at several sizes, soft broken internal marks, selective darker pigment edges, and small light paper gaps. Keep the existing overall tree silhouettes, branch layout, and positions. Break unit size and alignment; reduce uniform circular rims and plastic-looking center shading. Separate pale yellow-green upper clusters from gray-green lower pockets. Restore blossom highlights to warm pale coral/peach, with salmon pools and very little gray-purple. The current blossom palette is farther from the target than the baseline was.

2. **Fix ceiling/roof wash scale and stretching before adding more texture.** In round 1, the entrance ceiling has conspicuous long blue-white streaks radiating in depth; the forecourt and lookout contact views confirm this is a shared surface behavior. The target has broad, localized blue-gray cloudlike pools with irregular near-white paper between them, plus fine quiet paper tooth. Use non-stretched material coordinates or compensate for surface aspect ratio, then combine a few slow irregular pigment fields with low-contrast fine grain. The roof ribs can retain their directional structure; the wash itself should not turn into parallel stripes. Recheck entrance, forecourt, and lookout after this change.

3. **Retain the lighter wood, then make its marks directional and less uniformly mottled.** Round 1 improves the large posts and reconciles the very dark near-left wood with the rest of the building. Preserve that warmer/lighter direction. Add restrained vertical fibers on posts, horizontal marks on beams, selective pale warm reserves, and darker pooled edges only where useful. The target front beam has richer peach/ochre pigment variation than the comparatively even current beam. The study table and stairs in the contact sheet expose stretched or overly repetitive shared grain, so validate those as well. On the foreground floor, keep the new light peach areas, soften the dark slab seams, and exchange thin stretched streaks for broader warm wash forms and finer paper tooth.

4. **Replace the grass bands with soft, spatially grounded patches.** The target lawn has gentle yellow-green dry areas and broad gray-green shade beneath and to the left of the nearer tree. Round 1 adds variation but makes parallel bands and the front vertical edge conspicuous; this is especially visible in the entrance and approach views. Use irregular broad patches at differing scales and low contrast, then add soft local canopy/contact shade around the trunks and rocks. Reduce the blue-gray/cream camouflage effect and avoid a uniformly dark lawn perimeter. Test both spring and autumn before accepting shared ground changes.

5. **Use the remaining detail budget on bark and rocks after the large surfaces improve.** Tree trunks need broken warm vertical pigment and a little pale edge variation. Rocks should retain their chalky pale tone while their hard triangular light/dark boundaries are softened by irregular pigment. These are secondary to foliage, ceiling, and broad color structure; additional tiny noise alone will not resolve the main mismatch.

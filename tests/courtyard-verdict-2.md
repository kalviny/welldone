## Improvements

- The shared gaze correction visibly helps the architecture. In the common 1672 × 941 output, the screen base is now around the target's y≈480, the tree base is close to y≈500, and the stair foot is near y≈375 rather than round 1's y≈415. These are approximate visual landmarks, not a computed registration score.
- The stair, ceiling, deck, and study furniture have fewer evenly distributed pale scratches. They read more calmly at whole-image scale, which is closer to the target's hierarchy.
- Reflected posts and trunk are easier to identify than in round 1's milky pond. They carry useful dark cool value.
- The stone ring contains more varied shoulders and intermediate planes. It is less like a repeated set of white caps on flat blue side faces.

## Dominant remaining mismatches and regressions

**Foliage remains the first priority.** Removing the pointed pale flecks helped one symptom, but the final crown now reads as several large yellow solid pouches. The long lower rim left of the trunk and the large pouch to its right advertise closed geometry. Color variation exists inside them, but it does not create the target's layered leafy construction. The target has overlapping broad golden washes, irregular medium scallops, small breathing holes, a few warm/olive overlap pockets, and selective soft boundaries. Its internal values group into clusters; round 2 is comparatively uniform lemon/gold. Reintroducing dense bright specks would also miss the target.

**The new reflection segments look digital.** Stronger reflections are a useful improvement, but their edges now form repeated horizontal rectangular steps. The screen rails become a blocky ladder, and broad dark angular bars near the right and lower pond read as rigid geometric strips. In the target, identifiable thin rails and vertical post/trunk fragments survive inside irregular pigment gaps and softly uneven edges. Most interruptions are differences in opacity, edge shape, and local continuity; they do not share a repeated rectangular displacement shape. Round 1's continuous bending and round 2's stepped bending are different errors.

**The pond still has too little localized color structure.** Round 2 is mostly one medium pale cyan with a relatively smooth deeper near/right zone. The target places pale cream/sage illumination mainly toward the upper/left, translucent cool blue/green areas around it, and a substantially deeper mottled teal region toward the lower/right. Those regions interlock at several scales and let selected dark reflections emerge. The target does not distribute equal grain or equal darkness across the whole plane. The current dark reflection bars supply contrast, but not the same water-color structure.

**Calmer materials have not yet become painted materials.** The quieter wood is a gain, but broad warm pigment bands and occasional dry paper gaps are still subordinate to a generic cloudy surface. Rocks now have varied geometry, yet their gray-white values are fairly even around each dome; the target places larger luminous ivory reserves against selective cool-gray fragments. Fine paper grain should sit under a small number of broad washes and material-specific medium marks. More uniform noise would spend detail without addressing that hierarchy.

**The framing correction has a near-field cost.** The foreground center rock begins around y≈755 and is nearly fully visible in round 2; the target's counterpart begins around y≈805 and is clipped by the bottom. The large left and near-right stones are also roughly 30–45 pixels higher than the target. The newly aligned architectural landmarks should be preserved. A second whole-frame vertical camera adjustment would trade the current gain away.

## Priorities for the final bounded implementation round

1. **Replace the large pouch reading of the crown with overlapping medium wash clusters.** Keep its broad position and the removal of white needle flecks. Break long smooth undersides with asymmetric scallops and a few irregular gaps; vary visible cluster sizes instead of stacking similarly solid lobes. Group pale gold, ochre, and muted warm/olive pockets into larger patches with softer internal transitions. The result should read as leafy painted masses at whole-image scale before fine grain is considered.
2. **Make reflections irregular through pigment continuity and edge shape.** Preserve the newly useful cool trunk/post value, but remove the repeated rectangular horizontal steps. Use uneven gaps, varied short fragments, restrained lateral offsets, and softer edges. Keep screen rails narrow and partially legible; reduce the broad dark lower/right bars that dominate the current water. Judge the actual pond screenshot at full size, since a mathematically segmented shader can still look like digital stair steps.
3. **Give water and stone an explicit broad/medium/fine hierarchy.** Place localized cream/sage reserves at upper/left and deeper cyan/teal washes toward lower/right, with irregular medium patches connecting them. Keep high-frequency grain quieter. On stones, establish larger ivory planes and selective cool fractured patches, then let fine grain follow. Maintain enough object reflection contrast without globally darkening or whitening the pond.
4. **Carry the calmer treatment into richer shared timber and foliage color.** Keep the reduced scratchiness visible in the study and stair views. Add a limited number of longer warm timber washes and sparse pale gaps; retain quiet ivory plaster. In the shared autumn crowns, restore some grouped ochre variation without making every surface equally busy or every tree uniformly yellow.
5. **Treat remaining near-rock composition as a small final polish.** Preserve the current stair/tree/screen alignment. Compare the real foreground rock/pond proportions and near-field crop with the target, especially the center stone's lower clipping. Do not let another broad camera change consume the final round or undo the architectural improvement. The foliage and water work above have higher visual value.

## Whole-app consistency from the contact sheets

The round 2 changes visibly propagate through approach, bridge, gate, forecourt, garden, study, armory, stairs, and lookout. The bridge/gate paving, stair timber, ceilings, and study furniture are calmer and have fewer harsh white scratches. This is a useful global improvement. The tea view changes little and retains its coherent palette.

The crown regression is also global: approach and lookout trees read as stacked solid yellow cushions, while the closer forecourt/armory glimpses expose the same large lobes. The gate's paving is quieter but remains quite pale and cloudy. The study's quiet surfaces are easier to read than round 1, although the same lack of grouped middle-sized marks remains. These sheets show consistency of the treatment, not a verified reference match outside the supplied courtyard. They provide no functional or performance verdict.

## What improved

**Reflection continuity is the clearest gain.** Round 2's trunk and screen reflection formed a conspicuous ladder of sideways rectangular steps. In round 3, the vertical fragments connect more calmly, with thinner uneven interruptions and reduced opacity. Screen rails remain readable without the same strong checkerboard effect. This is materially closer to the target's irregular pigment continuity. However, broad angular dark fragments remain at the right side and near the lower pond; the result has not become a fully painted reflection.

**Water has more useful regional color.** The upper-left now contains a pale cream/sage area while the right is distinctly cooler blue-cyan. This starts to reproduce the target's placement of illumination. The target interlocks those regions with larger uneven teal pools and medium-scale pigment patches, especially in the foreground. Round 3 still blends smoothly across much of the plane, and its lower-right is too evenly bright and cyan.

**Near-field framing is closer.** The center foreground stone now begins near the target's lower position and is clipped by the bottom edge. The large left and near-right stones are also lower than in round 2. Their placement no longer creates the same elevated ring across the foreground. The architectural alignment remains essentially stable. This is a small composition gain, independent of whether the stones' material looks painted.

**The crown has more variation, but only a limited gain.** Compared with round 2, its undersides are broken into more overlapping bulges and its color contains more ochre pockets. It is less like a single pair of long yellow pouches. Nevertheless, the replacement shapes remain clearly closed, rounded masses. They have not acquired the target's construction from irregular leaf-wash clusters. This does not justify a large foliage score increase.

## Most important remaining gaps

1. **The tree still reads as solid lobes rather than layered golden leaf washes.** The target combines irregular medium scallops, broad translucent gold groups, sparse gaps, pale edges, and selective olive/ochre overlaps. Round 3 groups texture inside inflated forms, with long smooth lower contours and vertically streaked mottling. The canopy's left extent and broad mass distribution also differ. More small texture alone would not resolve that solid-volume reading.

2. **The pond remains too smooth and geometric at broad and medium scales.** Reflection stepping is substantially reduced, but the lower/right dark bar fragments still expose angular geometry. The target's darker teal foreground, cream/sage reserves, and cool translucent areas interlock through irregular wash patches. Round 3 has a comparatively even cyan field and soft gradient. It needs the target's hierarchy of regional washes, local gaps, and selective reflection strength to approach the reference closely.

3. **The shared surfaces lack the target's warm pigment hierarchy and paper reserves.** Target stones have large luminous ivory planes interrupted selectively by cool fractured patches; round 3 stones remain cloudy, fairly evenly mottled low-poly domes. Timber has coherent warmth but largely generic surface clouding, with limited long pigment bands and sparse paper-light breaks. Walls, stones, water, and foliage therefore share too much of the same procedural texture logic. The target varies the scale and placement of marks by material while retaining the same paper ground.

The foreground stones' lower placement is an improvement, but their shape and painted planes still differ. Floating leaves are a smaller remaining mismatch: the target's oval forms and shadows have more weight and edge character than the current flat pointed leaves.

## Whole-project consistency

The round 3 contact sheet retains a coherent warm timber, ivory plaster, blue roof, muted green, and autumn-gold palette across approach, bridge, gate, forecourt, tea, garden, study, armory, stairs, and lookout. The room views remain calm and legible. The garden's reflection and color changes fit that palette, and I do not see a broad stylistic regression in the other rooms.

The foliage change propagates visibly to approach, forecourt, armory, and lookout. More ochre variation is useful, but the repeated solid crown lobes remain apparent across those views. The lookout still exposes stacked broad crown layers. Study, tea, and stairs change little; their consistency is preserved, while their surfaces continue to show the same generic cloudy texture limitation. These sheets support a judgment of consistency across the project, not an exact reference match for views without supplied references.
